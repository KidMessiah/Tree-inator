Tree-inator feature tour

Every image below was rendered by the app's engine from the same settings you get in the window, using your CG2A canopy files as texture. Draw or generate a patch, pick a pattern and edge style, set the light, grade the layers, export a transparent PNG.

Left toolbar · Patch shape panel

Draw a shape, or generate one

Drag the Lasso to outline a forest; it grows the moment you let go. Lasso: cut clearing and the Polygon tools cut holes or make straight edges. Generate patch makes a random natural outline sized to fit the map, so you can skip drawing entirely. Green lines are the outlines; red is a cut.

Patch shape · Pattern

Realistic patch patterns

The pattern reshapes whatever outline you drew, so the forest is not a filled blob. Same outline in every image. Strength and Pattern size control how much and at what scale.

Patch shape · Preset

Presets

A preset sets the pattern, coverage, gaps and lone trees in one go. Each only changes the settings that define its look, so your colour and light settings stay.

Shape and edges

Realistic or blurred edges

Realistic breaks the edge into grass, shrubs, lone trees and shadows that sit well on a background. Blurred gives a continuous canopy with a soft feather and no shadow footprint, like CG2A's Zero Edge Fill, so overlapping patches merge without a seam.

Layers and colour

Seven layers, each with its own shadow

The forest is built bottom to top. Canopy forms patches you can see through to the grass and shrubs below, and every layer is shaded by the ones above it. Each can be hidden or graded on its own. Click a layer to see it alone.

Light and shadow

Shadow direction, length, softness, or none

Drag the dial in the app (the yellow dot is the sun) or use the UL, UR, DL, DR buttons. Crown shading and cast shadows always agree. Pick a direction:

Down right is CG2A's default. Shadows land on the layers below, and on empty ground outside the patch.

Layers and colour · Global grading

Colour grading per layer

Every layer has opacity, brightness, contrast, saturation, hue and tint, and a global grade sits on top. Shadow colour and opacity are separate. These looks are all from the same forest:

Canopy

Canopy controls

Coverage sets how much of the inside is canopy versus see-through patches. Crown size and Lumpiness change the trees themselves; the lumpiness pair is shown at full resolution.

Undergrowth, grass, lone trees

Grassy edges, lone trees and haze

Close-ups of the lower edge of the patch. The grass fringe thins into specks, lone trees sit in small groups with long shadows, and a faint haze reaches furthest out.

Left toolbar · Trees and brushes panel

Tree brush

Paint trees by hand on top of, or instead of, the generator. Tree brush scatters trees under the cursor with spacing so they never pile up. Single tree places one per click. Tree eraser removes trees. Pick the species and the layer (canopy, shrubs, lone trees, pebbles); the layer sets the height, so painted trees cast and receive shadows like generated ones. [ and ] resize the brush, and undo covers every stroke.

Editing a generated forest

Painting replaces the generated trees under the brush, so what you paint is never hidden. The eraser cuts trails and clearings; the grass underneath stays.

Fallback switches

Generated trees and Painted trees turn each source on or off. With both on and nothing painted, the output is pixel-identical to the generator alone, so the original system is always there as a fallback.

Trees and brushes · Species editor

Species

Each species has its own crown recipe and colour: how pointed, how lumpy, lump size and spread, branch spokes, height (which sets shadow length), brightness, saturation and tint. Seven come built in: Broadleaf, Conifer, Sage, Autumn, Golden, Dead and Bush. Duplicate one to make your own. Broadleaf is the original recipe.

Species mix in generated canopy

The generator can mix a second species into the canopy in clumps, like the lighter patches in the CG2A files. Amount sets how much, Clump size how big the patches are.

Prototype · not in the app yet

Our own texturing engine

Today the leaf detail is copied from the CG2A files and recoloured. The prototype builds it from scratch instead: every crown is covered in hundreds of tiny leaf clusters, each a small bump with its own colour, lit by the same sun. Species then control leaf shape (round clusters or needles), leaf size and a real colour ramp, and dead trees get a branch skeleton instead of recoloured leaves. CG2A texturing stays as the backup.

Next step: build this into the engine as a third colouring mode next to "CG2A texture bank" and "Palette", chosen per species.

Canopy · Texture bank

CG2A texture vs palette

Preview and export

Transparent PNG export

Exports are straight-alpha PNGs at the map size times Export scale. Set Map size to your battlemap's pixel size and the file drops in at 0,0. Tick any mix of forest.png, forest_noshadow.png and forest_shadow.png. A full 2048 × 1536 forest renders in about a second.

Overlay panel · Top bar

Overlay mode for tracing

This one lives in the window, so there is no render to show. It makes the app see-through and always on top, so you can trace an area of a map open in another program.

  1. Open your map in Dungeondraft, Photoshop or Inkarnate.
  2. In Tree-inator set Map size to the map's pixel size, and Zoom % to the zoom the other program shows, so sizes match.
  3. Tick Overlay mode. Lower Window opacity until you can see the map through it. Press H to hide the panels; a small floating toolbar stays.
  4. Line the window up over the map and trace with the lasso.
  5. Press Ctrl+Shift+T to let clicks pass through to the program underneath. Press it again to draw in Tree-inator. It works even when the other program has focus.
  6. Export, then place the PNG at 0,0 in your map.

Window opacity uses a Windows setting not yet tested with this app's graphics mode. If the window stays solid, tell me and I'll switch the method.
